SINDy Module - STEM VISUALIZATION

How do I compare trained models?

Every training session is saved in the History Leaderboard. Start with validation RMSE and \(R^2\), but also compare equation sparsity, residual structure, and whether the terms make physical sense. Then select the same Run ID in Test and evaluate it on a genuinely new trajectory.

A strong candidate is usually simple, has similar train/validation performance, produces unstructured residuals, and remains accurate during forward simulation.

Why are Train and Test metrics different?

Train and validation metrics are computed in derivative space, \(dX/dt\), because that is what SINDy fits directly. Test metrics are computed in state space, \(x(t)\), after integrating the discovered equations.

The values have different units and should not be compared numerically. Small derivative errors can accumulate during integration, so always inspect both kinds of evidence.

Why did the threshold remove every term?

The sparsity threshold is larger than every fitted coefficient, so STLSQ pruned the full library and returned \(dX/dt=0\). Lower the threshold, preferably in small increments using the exact-threshold input, and retrain.

Do not automatically choose the smallest threshold: an overly small value can preserve many noisy terms. Look for the simplest equation that still validates and simulates well.

Why does a good model still contain junk terms?

Random Sampling can place nearly identical neighboring timesteps in training and validation. Because a trajectory is autocorrelated, this leakage can make validation look better than the discovered structure really is.

Try Random Block, inspect Ensemble inclusion, and compare the actual equations—not only the aggregate score. Spurious terms often have low inclusion or unstable coefficients.

How should I use multiple training files?

Upload trajectories from the same dynamical system under different initial conditions. Every file must use identical state-column names and order, with time in the first column.

The module differentiates each trajectory separately before pooling the training pairs. This avoids a false derivative jump where one file ends and the next restarts at \(t=0\), while constraining one equation to explain a broader region of state space.

Why is R² high but simulation diverges?

Training \(R^2\) measures how well the model reproduces local derivatives, not the complete trajectory. Slight coefficient errors are repeatedly integrated and can accumulate into phase drift, amplitude error, or instability.

Use the Test tab to evaluate \(x(t)\) directly, inspect where divergence begins, and prefer equations that remain stable across unseen initial conditions.

Why can solve_ivp fail on test data?

SINDy only learns the region explored by its training trajectories. A test initial condition far outside that region forces the equation to extrapolate, and high-degree polynomial terms can grow extremely quickly.

Test with meaningfully different—but not wildly out-of-range—initial conditions. If integration still fails, try a simpler library, lower polynomial degree, or stronger sparsity.

Why does prediction drift over time?

Forward prediction repeatedly feeds the model’s output back into itself. Small errors in frequency, damping, or nonlinear coefficients therefore accumulate. Oscillators are especially sensitive: a small frequency error eventually becomes a large pointwise phase error.

Judge both short- and long-horizon behavior. A model can be scientifically useful over a finite horizon even when it is not an indefinite predictor.

What does term inclusion percentage mean?

Inclusion is the fraction of successful bootstrap fits in which a candidate term remains non-zero after thresholding. For example, 82% means the term survived in 82 of every 100 successful resampled fits.

High inclusion suggests structural robustness, but it is not proof of physical truth. Also inspect coefficient spread, sign consistency, validation performance, and domain knowledge.

Why is a coefficient CI unavailable?

This module reports a 95% bootstrap coefficient interval only when a term appears in strictly more than 50% of successful fits. A term selected half the time has no structural majority, so an interval computed only from its non-zero values would look more trustworthy than it really is.

The CI is conditional on the term being present. Read it together with inclusion and stability.

Original vs consensus equation?

The original equation comes from the selected training run. The consensus equation keeps only terms whose bootstrap inclusion exceeds the chosen consensus threshold and uses their bootstrap-mean coefficients.

Agreement between the two is evidence of robustness. Disagreement identifies terms that depend strongly on the sampled data; it does not automatically mean the original equation is wrong.

Why can Time-based split have lower R² but cleaner equations?

Damped systems concentrate useful nonlinear information in the early, high-amplitude region. Time-based splitting puts that region in training, which can reveal cleaner terms. Its validation tail may have very low signal variance, making \(R^2\) poor even when absolute errors are small.

A lower tail \(R^2\) does not automatically imply worse equations. Inspect absolute error, equation structure, residuals, and performance on a new initial condition.

How do I model an explicitly time-dependent system?

The current workflow assumes an autonomous system, \(dX/dt=f(X)\). For a forcing term such as \(\sin(t)\), add time as an auxiliary state \(\tau\) with \(d\tau/dt=1\). The augmented system can then represent \(\sin(\tau)\) through the Fourier or Combined library.

Include the auxiliary state as a normal CSV column and keep the same column order across every trajectory.

Should I trust a model when every metric looks good?

No single score is sufficient. Look for agreement across several kinds of evidence:

  • Train and validation performance are reasonably close.
  • Residuals have little visible time structure or unexplained frequency content.
  • The equation is sparse and physically plausible.
  • A new trajectory is reproduced through forward simulation.
  • Important terms remain stable under the Ensemble bootstrap.

Use the module to assemble evidence—not to replace scientific judgment.
